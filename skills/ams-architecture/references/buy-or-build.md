# Buy a commercial AMS, build one, or combine both

Last checked: 2026-10-05

This file names no products, and does not compare or rank them.

## What it covers

This file helps you decide whether to buy a commercial athlete management system, build your own, or combine both. It lists the questions to ask a vendor, and the exit plan to agree on before you sign.

## Method

### Compare the full cost

Compare the full cost of each choice over a period you choose, such as three years, not the license price alone:

| Cost | Buy | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Money | License fees, setup fees, paid integrations | Tool licenses, hosting, any paid help |
| Staff time to set up | Configuration, data migration, training | Design, building, testing, documentation |
| Staff time each week | Checking imports, managing users | Imports, fixes, changes, updates, backups |
| Risk when staff leave | Lower, because the vendor supports the system. Lock-in and setup knowledge remain risks. | High, unless the handover document and a second person are in place |
| Fit to your questions | Limited to what the product offers and allows you to export | Can fit your questions exactly |

Torres-Ronda and Schelling (2017) give a guideline for vetting a technology before a sport organization adopts it, and for putting it in place. Schelling and Robertson (2020) include feasibility, with legal checks, in their framework for building a decision support system.

### Know when each choice fits

Buying usually fits these cases:

- Many teams or sports need one system.
- Medical records need features, such as a record of every view and change, that are hard to build and maintain yourself.
- Athletes need a phone app for forms and their own results.
- No staff member has hours each week to maintain a build.

Building usually fits these cases:

- A small staff asks specific questions that commercial products do not answer.
- A staff member has the skills and the time to maintain it, and a second person can cover.
- The budget does not cover a license.

### Consider combining both

Some organizations buy a commercial AMS for athlete forms, medical records, and device links, and build their own analysis on top of its export or API. If you combine both, confirm that the product lets you export all your data, in an open format, on a schedule.

### Ask a vendor these questions

Ask every vendor these questions before you sign:

- Can we export all our data, at any time, in an open format such as CSV, without an extra fee?
- Is there an API? Is it included in the price? Where is its documentation?
- Which device and form integrations exist? Who fixes them when a device vendor changes its export?
- How does the product calculate each metric? Can we see the formula and change it?
- Where is the data hosted? Which subcontractors and countries handle it?
- Which agreements will you sign, such as a data processing agreement, a business associate agreement if HIPAA applies, or terms that let a school share student records with you under FERPA?
- Do you use our data, including de-identified data, to train models or build products?
- How fast will you tell us about a breach?
- Which access roles exist? Can we limit coaches to their own team and keep medical data separate?
- Who owns the data? What happens to it when the contract ends?
- How is the price set: per athlete, per team, per user, or per feature?
- How long does setup take, and who does the work?
- Which organizations like ours can we speak to?

### Agree on the exit plan before you sign

Confirm in the contract how you get all your data out, in which format, and how long the vendor keeps it after the contract ends. Run a test export during the trial. If you cannot get the data out, you cannot leave.

### Find products to compare

Ask staff at similar programs which products they use, and search for athlete management systems. Treat any list as incomplete. Compare products against your decision list and the vendor questions above, not against each other's feature lists.

## Common mistakes

These are the mistakes AI tools and staff make most often with this decision:

- Comparing the license price with zero. A build costs staff time every week.
- Building because it seems free, then losing the system when the builder leaves.
- Buying without testing a full data export.
- Accepting a vendor's metric without seeing how it is calculated.
- Recommending one product as the best. Fit depends on the organization's needs, staff, and budget.

## Check the result

Run these checks on the recommendation:

- Confirm the comparison covers money, set-up time, weekly time, and staff-departure risk, over the same period for each choice.
- Confirm the plan includes the vendor questions and a test export.
- Confirm the recommendation names no product as the best.
